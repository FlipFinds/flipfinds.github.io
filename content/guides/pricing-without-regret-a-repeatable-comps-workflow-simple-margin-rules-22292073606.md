## Build a price estimate from evidence

Research the exact model or edition and compare condition, included parts and testing. Start with completed sales when available. An active listing is useful for comparing competition, but cannot establish that a buyer paid the asking price.

## Compare the buyer's whole transaction

Read item price and shipping separately. A $35 item with $12 shipping has a different buyer cost from a $35 free-shipping sale. Bundles, accepted offers and missing transaction details can make a displayed price uncertain.

Exclude results that do not match your item. A different variant, a sealed example or a working device is not interchangeable with a damaged or untested one. If the evidence is sparse, use a range and state what remains unknown.

## Choose a defensible planning value

Use a price you can explain from the comparable evidence. Do not call a narrow range precise when it comes from one uncertain sale. Condition differences and missing parts may justify a lower estimate or more research.

For a hypothetical item with comparable sales around $35-$45, testing the lower end can show whether costs still leave your chosen profit. This is an example of a conservative check, not a guaranteed price band.

## Check your cost floor

Enter purchase cost, seller fees, shipping you pay, packaging and other known costs in the [resale calculator](/calculator/). Use the [break-even tool](/calculator/break-even/) to see where modeled profit reaches zero.

A cost floor answers what your expenses require; market evidence answers what buyers may pay. If those do not overlap, increasing the asking price does not establish demand. Review the assumptions or acknowledge that the purchase may not deliver the planned return.

## Evaluate offers and record actual outcomes

Recalculate at the accepted offer and the actual shipping terms. Check fee thresholds and avoid counting the same label twice. After the sale, replace planning assumptions with actual proceeds and charges.
