A sourcing estimate is only as useful as the questions behind it. Use this checklist with an item you can inspect and a sale estimate you can explain.

## Identify and inspect

Confirm the model, edition or size. Look for missing parts, damage and testing requirements. Research genuinely similar completed sales rather than copying the highest current asking price.

Pause when authenticity or functionality remains uncertain. A photo or barcode suggestion is a starting point, not certification.

## Separate the selling costs

Estimate seller fees for your marketplace, account and category. Distinguish buyer-paid shipping from the label you pay for. Add packaging and other foreseeable expenses, including repair costs only when you have a defensible allowance.

Large or fragile items deserve a package check before buying. Weight, dimensions and protective material can change the cost materially.

## Choose a target and solve backward

Use a personal dollar-profit or ROI target. With a hypothetical $40 sale, $4 fee, $6 shipping and $1 supplies, $29 remains before purchase cost. A $15 profit target leaves a $14 maximum buy cost.

Try a lower sale estimate or higher shipping amount. If the buying ceiling disappears with a small plausible change, treat the decision as uncertain rather than presenting the first result as a safe price.

## Record the decision

Save the actual purchase cost and identifying details when you buy. Update the status and actual outcome later. The [maximum buy price tool](/calculator/max-buy-price/) does the arithmetic, but cannot predict demand, authenticity, returns or your time cost.

Use manual entry if assisted research is unavailable. Keep connected research and optional Cloud requirements separate from the local inventory workflow.
