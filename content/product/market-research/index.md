The current FlipFinds mobile app and dashboard do not include a separate Research workspace. Use the external marketplace checks below, then enter your own list price in the item record.

A listing price answers what a seller hopes to receive. A completed sale is stronger evidence of what a buyer paid, though it still needs context. Use marketplace websites for that research, then record your chosen list price and actual costs in FlipFinds.

## Match the item before comparing prices

Start with the exact model, edition or identifier. Compare condition, included parts and whether the item has been tested. Exclude bundles or variants that do not describe what you are selling.

Read shipping terms separately. A $35 item with $12 shipping is not the same buyer cost as a $35 item with free shipping. An accepted offer or incomplete result may leave the actual price uncertain.

## Use active listings for competition

Active results can show how other sellers describe or position an item. They do not establish sell-through, guaranteed demand or the price you will receive. Research coverage varies and some items have too little comparable history to justify a precise estimate.

For a hypothetical item with comparable completed sales around $35-$45, start your cost calculation with a conservative price you can explain. If your example is damaged or missing parts, do not apply the cleanest comparable without adjustment.

## Return to the item decision

Enter your chosen estimate in the [maximum buy price tool](/calculator/max-buy-price/) or the relevant marketplace calculator. Save the actual purchase cost in FlipFinds, and later compare the estimate with the recorded sale.

FlipFinds does not independently certify marketplace data or automatically synchronize sold results into every item. External research can require connectivity and a separate browser session. Keep uncertainty visible rather than turning a sparse result into a confident pricing claim.
