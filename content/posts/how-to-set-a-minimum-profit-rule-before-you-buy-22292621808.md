A minimum profit rule is a planning target you choose before sourcing. It does not prove demand or turn a risky item into a safe purchase.

## Write down the costs before the target

Start with a researched sale estimate. Subtract selling fees, shipping, supplies and foreseeable extra costs. Leave purchase cost separate: the amount remaining after your profit target is the most you can pay under those assumptions.

For a hypothetical $40 sale, $4 fee, $6 shipping and $1 packaging leave $29 before buying the item. Choosing $15 profit produces a $14 buying ceiling. Choosing $20 leaves a $9 ceiling. Neither target is a universal reseller benchmark.

## Check uncertainty before using the ceiling

Compare condition and included parts with sold examples. If the likely sale price is lower, rerun the estimate. Add repair costs only when you have a defensible amount. Unknown authenticity or an untested fault may require more research rather than another guessed allowance.

## Compare dollars and ROI deliberately

A dollar target describes money left after entered expenses. ROI compares that profit with the cash invested. If your budget is limited, you may care about both; time and how long inventory sits still matter and are not automatically modeled.

Use the [maximum buy price calculator](/calculator/max-buy-price/) to test your own target. Save the actual purchase cost in the item record, then compare the eventual sale with the original decision.
