Small shipping and fee differences can consume a planned profit. Audit the transaction assumptions before treating an estimate as spendable money.

## Compare like with like

Write down item price, shipping collected from the buyer, the label you pay for, packaging and seller charges. Shipping collected belongs on the revenue side; a label belongs on the expense side. Marketplace-collected tax is not seller revenue, though some fees include it in their calculation base.

Use the marketplace's current terms for your country, category and account. A general preset cannot know promoted-listing charges, seller discounts or every account-specific fee.

## Test the package

Estimate weight and packed dimensions with the packaging you will actually use. An unboxed item may need protective material that changes both size and label cost. Check fragile or bulky stock before accepting a price based on the smallest possible parcel.

## Reconcile after a completed sale

Compare the item record with the selling statement. Replace estimates with actual charges, including a shipping discount or an additional expense. Do not count the same label again in Other costs.

For a hypothetical sale that leaves $18 before postage, a $12 label and $1 packaging leave $5 before unentered expenses. That is why the fee estimate and package check belong before the offer decision.
