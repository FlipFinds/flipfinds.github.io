A weekly review is useful when it distinguishes recorded activity from an incomplete history. Start with item counts and actual sales you can verify.

## Define the period and population

Decide whether you are reviewing a starting inventory cohort or sales against items available during a period. Those denominators differ. Do not divide a week's sales by today's remaining stock and call it a comparable sell-through rate.

If listing dates or opening inventory are missing, report the sold count and current unsold count separately. A descriptive count is more trustworthy than a percentage with an invented denominator.

## Choose a small review set

Look at items recorded as sold, items waiting to be listed and older listings needing attention. Confirm that statuses match reality before comparing the groups. Select a manageable batch rather than setting a universal age threshold for every category.

## Pick one next action

A listing with unclear condition may need better photos. An unlisted item needs preparation. A completed sale with missing shipping needs reconciliation. These are different problems and should not all trigger a price cut.

Save the action with the item and review the same definitions next week. Low volume and partial periods can make apparent trends unstable. FlipFinds can help you keep the records, but the underlying history determines what the review can support.
