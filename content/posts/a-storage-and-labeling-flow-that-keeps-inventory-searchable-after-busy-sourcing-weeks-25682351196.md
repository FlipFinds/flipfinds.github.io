A storage label is useful when it leads to the correct item quickly. Complicated location codes do not help if they are never updated.

## Choose a stable item identifier

Use a short identifier that is unique in your own records. Put it on the item's bag or tag where it will not damage the product. Keep the identifier stable even if you move the item.

Separately record the current shelf, bin or area in the tracking system you use. Location describes where the item is now; identity describes which item it is.

## Keep similar stock distinguishable

Include size, model, condition and useful photos in the record. Two shirts from the same brand or two versions of a game may require more detail than a broad title. Verify the identifier when packing a sale.

## Update moves at the point of handling

When you relocate stock, update its location before moving to the next batch. Mark completed sales promptly so empty bags or missing items do not look like unexplained inventory losses.

For a hypothetical pair of similar jackets, separate identifiers prevent the cost or condition of one from being attached to the other's sale. The method works in a spreadsheet or your item records; it does not require a marketplace integration.

Start with one bin and test whether another person could locate an item from the record alone. Expand only after that basic lookup works.
