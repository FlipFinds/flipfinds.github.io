A weekly cleanup makes records easier to trust. It should fix specific discrepancies before you draw conclusions about sales or sourcing.

## Reconcile a small batch with reality

Compare recorded items with the physical stock and completed transactions. Correct sold items still shown as available. Review possible duplicates before deleting anything; similar titles can describe different stock.

Fill in purchase costs you can substantiate. An unknown cost is not the same as a free item and should not silently become zero in a profit review.

## Separate data cleanup from listing work

An incomplete record may need a cost or identifier. An unsold item may need better presentation or research. A missing shipping charge needs reconciliation. Group the issues by next action instead of applying the same discount to every item.

Choose a batch that fits your time. There is no supported universal number of minutes, age cutoff or margin percentage that makes every reseller's records complete.

## Leave a useful stopping point

Record what changed and what still needs evidence. Pick one next action for the coming week, then revisit the same records. If your history is partial, describe that limit when reviewing analytics.

The goal is to make the next item decision easier to explain. A clean record does not guarantee sell-through, but it reduces the chance that stale status or missing costs drive a misleading conclusion.
