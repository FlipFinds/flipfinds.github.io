At a thrift store or garage sale, the biggest missing information is often what the item is, what condition it is in and what it will cost to sell. A quick estimate is useful only after those questions have reasonable answers.

## Make a short field checklist

Look for the exact model or edition. Check damage, missing parts and whether testing is possible. Consider the packed size and shipping risk before using a sale estimate for a fragile or bulky item.

Barcode/photo-assisted entry can help start an item record where supported. Review the suggestion and use manual entry when a lookup is unavailable. Connectivity and service coverage can limit research away from home.

## Set your buying ceiling before negotiating

Use the [maximum buy price tool](/calculator/max-buy-price/) with a researched sale estimate and your own costs and target. If a $40 expected sale leaves $29 after fees, shipping and supplies, a $15 profit target means a $14 maximum purchase cost under those assumptions.

That ceiling does not include your time, taxes or uncertain repair costs unless you enter an allowance. It does not guarantee that the item will sell. Unknown authenticity or untested functionality may justify more research instead of a confident price.

## Record the purchase while it is fresh

Save what you paid and enough identifying details to find the item later. At home, complete research and listing preparation from the inventory record. Update the actual sale when it happens.
